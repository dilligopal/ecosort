    Classification Taxonomy

    Supported Waste Categories

    EcoSort recognises six primary municipal waste streams — each with tailored disposal and recycling guidance.

    Recyclable

    Plastic

    Bottles, food packaging, rigid containers, PET & HDPE items.

    • Rinse and empty containers thoroughly before disposal.
    • Check resin code (1–7) against local curbside rules.
    • Keep plastic films separate — use supermarket drop-offs.
    Recyclable

    Paper

    Newspapers, office paper, cardboard boxes, clean packaging.

    • Flatten boxes to save collection bin space.
    • Keep paper completely dry and uncontaminated.
    • Greasy food wrappers and pizza boxes go in general waste.
    Recyclable

    Metal

    Aluminium cans, food tins, steel scrap, clean foil, bottle caps.

    • Rinse cans; aluminium is infinitely recyclable.
    • Wrap sharp lids safely before disposal.
    • Take large scrap to a municipal drop-off site.
    Recyclable

    Glass

    Clear, amber & green bottles, jars, condiment containers.

    • Rinse and remove caps before recycling.
    • Do not mix Pyrex, ceramics, or mirrors with glass.
    • Wrap broken glass in paper before disposal.
    Compostable

    Organic Waste

    Vegetable peels, fruit scraps, eggshells, coffee grounds, garden clippings.

    • Compost at home or use municipal organic bins.
    • Avoid meat, dairy, or cooked oils in backyard compost.
    • Diverts methane-generating waste from landfill.
    Special Handling

    Electronic Waste

    Cables, chargers, phones, PCBs, batteries, small electronics.

    • NEVER bin e-waste in ordinary recycling or trash.
    • Use certified e-waste or manufacturer take-back centres.
    • Tape battery terminals and factory-reset devices first.

    Regional Note: Sorting rules differ by city and country. When in doubt, consult your local waste management authority or check the item's material symbol.

    About The Project

    Bridging Computer Vision & Environmental Action

    Municipal solid waste management is a global crisis. Contaminated recycling streams result in tonnes of recyclable material being sent to landfill every day — not because of a shortage of facilities, but because of incorrect sorting at source.

    EcoSort demonstrates how lightweight convolutional neural networks can automate waste identification at zero marginal cost to consumers. Developed as an academic initiative in the Department of Electronics and Communication Engineering, the project unifies matrix transformations, convolutional feature maps, softmax classification, and full-stack web engineering into a single accessible tool.

    Academic Objectives

    • Feature Extraction: Evaluate CNN feature maps on diverse, real-world waste textures.
    • Edge Deployment: Design lightweight inference compatible with Render's free CPU tier.
    • Responsible UX: Translate softmax probabilities into safe, actionable guidance.
    • Honest Uncertainty: Flag low-confidence predictions rather than fabricate results.
